# Practical Exercises: Chapter 1 – Journal Entries

**Course:** Financial Accounting (First-Year B.Com)  
**Platform:** Learn Accounting  
**Framework to Apply:** Traditional Approach (Personal, Real, and Nominal Accounts + Three Golden Rules)  
**Business Context:** All five exercises belong to the books of **Kiran Textiles & Garments** (Proprietor: Ms. Kiran Sharma, Jaipur) for **March 2026** (Financial Year ended March 31, 2026). All transactions exclude GST.

---

## Instructions for Students

1. Attempt every exercise independently using the **5-Step Journalising Method** learned in the lesson before checking [exercise-solutions.md](file:///C:/Users/Admin/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/learn-accounting/docs/content/exercise-solutions.md).
2. Classify every account strictly under the **Traditional Approach**, specifying its exact subtype where applicable (*Natural / Artificial / Representative Personal*; *Tangible / Intangible Real*; or *Nominal*).
3. Present every journal entry in proper standard format (`Date | Particulars | L.F. | Debit (₹) | Credit (₹)`) with a clear **Narration** starting with `(Being ...)`.
4. Always confirm that `Total Debit (₹) = Total Credit (₹)`.

## Exercise 1 – Easy: Goods Sold for Cash

**Date:** March 4, 2026  
**Transaction Details:** Kiran Textiles & Garments sold cotton fabric goods for cash amounting to ₹32,000 to walk-in retail customers.

### Questions for Students
1. Identify the two accounts involved in this transaction.
2. Classify each account under the Traditional Approach (mentioning the subtype where applicable).
3. Which Golden Rules of Accounting apply to these two accounts, and which account should be debited and credited?
4. Record the complete Journal Entry in standard format along with a proper narration.

> **Hint:** Think about what physical asset enters the cash counter and what revenue account records the sale of trading goods.

## Exercise 3 – Moderate: Machinery Purchased Through Bank

**Date:** March 14, 2026  
**Transaction Details:** Purchased a heavy-duty embroidery machine worth ₹1,40,000 from Bharat Machinery Works for long-term use in the garment workshop, and paid the full amount immediately through an NEFT bank transfer.

### Questions for Students
1. Identify the two accounts involved in this transaction. *(Explain briefly why neither `Purchases A/c` nor `Bharat Machinery Works A/c` is used.)*
2. Classify each account under the Traditional Approach (including subtypes).
3. Apply the relevant Golden Rules of Accounting to determine the Debit and Credit treatment.
4. Prepare the properly formatted Journal Entry with a clear narration.

> **Hint:** Distinguish between *trading goods* (for resale) and *fixed assets* (for long-term business use), and remember the Traditional classification of `Bank A/c`.

## Exercise 5 – Challenging: Depreciation and Goods Withdrawn by the Owner for Personal Use

**Date:** March 31, 2026  
**Transaction Details:** At the close of the financial year ended March 31, 2026, record the following two year-end transactions in the books of Kiran Textiles & Garments:
- **Part A (Depreciation):** Provide annual depreciation at **10% per annum** on shop furniture having a book value of ₹80,000 (used for the full financial year).
- **Part B (Drawings of Goods):** Ms. Kiran Sharma (the proprietor) withdrew ready-made garments (trading goods) costing ₹6,500 from the shop for her personal family use.

### Questions for Students
1. For **Part A**, calculate the exact depreciation amount in ₹. Then identify the two accounts involved and classify them under the Traditional Approach.
2. For **Part B**, identify the two accounts involved, classify them under the Traditional Approach, and explain why `Sales A/c` is **not** credited.
3. State the Golden Rules of Accounting applied to **both** Part A and Part B.
4. Pass two separate, properly formatted Journal Entries (one for Part A and one for Part B) with clear narrations, and verify that both entries are mathematically balanced.

> **Hint:**
> - For Part A, depreciation is a non-cash loss/expense that directly reduces the value of the Tangible Real Asset.
> - For Part B, apply the Business Entity Concept and recall how the cost of goods purchased for resale is reduced when the owner takes goods at cost price.
