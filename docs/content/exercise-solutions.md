## Solution to Exercise 1 – Easy: Goods Sold for Cash

- **Date:** March 4, 2026
- **Transaction:** Sold cotton fabric goods for cash ₹32,000 to walk-in retail customers.

### 1. Accounts Involved
1. **Cash A/c**
2. **Sales A/c**

### 2. Account Classification (Traditional Approach)
| Account Name | Account Type | Subtype |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cash A/c** | Real Account | Tangible Real Account |
| **Sales A/c** | Nominal Account | Revenue / Income |

### 3. Step-by-Step Solution & Golden Rules Applied
- **Step 1 (Identify Nature):** Goods are sold for immediate cash payment, so Cash is received and Sales revenue is earned.
- **Step 2 (Apply Real Account Rule to Cash A/c):** *Debit what comes in.* Cash of ₹32,000 comes into the business → **Debit Cash A/c ₹32,000**.
- **Step 3 (Apply Nominal Account Rule to Sales A/c):** *Credit all incomes and gains.* Sales is the primary revenue/income of the business → **Credit Sales A/c ₹32,000**.
- **Step 4 (Balance Check):** Total Debit (₹32,000) = Total Credit (₹32,000).

### 4. Correct Journal Entry

| Date | Particulars | L.F. | Debit (₹) | Credit (₹) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2026 Mar 4 | Cash A/c &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dr.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To Sales A/c<br>*(Being cotton fabric goods sold for cash)* | | 32,000 | <br>32,000 |

- **Proper Narration:** *(Being cotton fabric goods sold for cash)*
- **Key Takeaway:** Always use `Sales A/c` (never "Goods A/c") when selling items that the business regularly trades in.

## Solution to Exercise 2 – Easy: Payment to a Creditor

- **Date:** March 9, 2026
- **Transaction:** Paid ₹19,500 in cash to Mr. Ramesh Verma (a supplier/creditor) against an earlier credit purchase of yarn.

### 1. Accounts Involved
1. **Ramesh Verma's A/c**
2. **Cash A/c**

### 2. Account Classification (Traditional Approach)
| Account Name | Account Type | Subtype |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ramesh Verma's A/c** | Personal Account | Natural Personal Account |
| **Cash A/c** | Real Account | Tangible Real Account |

### 3. Step-by-Step Solution & Golden Rules Applied
- **Step 1 (Identify Nature):** The purchase was already recorded earlier when yarn was bought on credit; this transaction only settles the amount owed to Mr. Ramesh Verma. Therefore, `Purchases A/c` is **not** affected.
- **Step 2 (Apply Personal Account Rule to Ramesh Verma's A/c):** *Debit the receiver.* Mr. Ramesh Verma receives ₹19,500 from the business → **Debit Ramesh Verma's A/c ₹19,500**.
- **Step 3 (Apply Real Account Rule to Cash A/c):** *Credit what goes out.* Cash of ₹19,500 goes out of the business → **Credit Cash A/c ₹19,500**.
- **Step 4 (Balance Check):** Total Debit (₹19,500) = Total Credit (₹19,500).

### 4. Correct Journal Entry

| Date | Particulars | L.F. | Debit (₹) | Credit (₹) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2026 Mar 9 | Ramesh Verma's A/c &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dr.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To Cash A/c<br>*(Being cash paid to creditor Mr. Ramesh Verma against earlier credit purchase)* | | 19,500 | <br>19,500 |

- **Proper Narration:** *(Being cash paid to creditor Mr. Ramesh Verma against earlier credit purchase)*
- **Key Takeaway:** When paying off a creditor, always debit the creditor's Personal Account to reduce the amount payable to them.

## Solution to Exercise 3 – Moderate: Machinery Purchased Through Bank

- **Date:** March 14, 2026
- **Transaction:** Purchased a heavy-duty embroidery machine worth ₹1,40,000 from Bharat Machinery Works for long-term workshop use and paid immediately via NEFT bank transfer.

### 1. Accounts Involved
1. **Machinery A/c**
2. **Bank A/c**

*(Why not `Purchases A/c` or `Bharat Machinery Works A/c`?)*
- **Why not `Purchases A/c`:** Kiran Textiles & Garments deals in textiles, not machinery. The embroidery machine is a long-term fixed asset bought for business use, not goods for resale, so `Machinery A/c` is debited.
- **Why not `Bharat Machinery Works A/c`:** Because full payment was made immediately via NEFT bank transfer, it is a settled bank transaction—no creditor balance arises with Bharat Machinery Works.

### 2. Account Classification (Traditional Approach)
| Account Name | Account Type | Subtype |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Machinery A/c** | Real Account | Tangible Real Account |
| **Bank A/c** | Personal Account | Artificial Personal Account |

### 3. Step-by-Step Solution & Golden Rules Applied
- **Step 1 (Identify Nature):** A physical fixed asset (embroidery machine) enters the business, and payment is made by the bank via NEFT.
- **Step 2 (Apply Real Account Rule to Machinery A/c):** *Debit what comes in.* Machinery worth ₹1,40,000 comes into the business → **Debit Machinery A/c ₹1,40,000**.
- **Step 3 (Apply Personal Account Rule to Bank A/c):** *Credit the giver.* The bank (an artificial person) pays ₹1,40,000 on behalf of the business → **Credit Bank A/c ₹1,40,000**.
- **Step 4 (Balance Check):** Total Debit (₹1,40,000) = Total Credit (₹1,40,000).

### 4. Correct Journal Entry

| Date | Particulars | L.F. | Debit (₹) | Credit (₹) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2026 Mar 14 | Machinery A/c &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dr.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To Bank A/c<br>*(Being embroidery machinery purchased and paid through NEFT bank transfer)* | | 1,40,000 | <br>1,40,000 |

- **Proper Narration:** *(Being embroidery machinery purchased and paid through NEFT bank transfer)*
- **Key Takeaway:** Fixed assets are always debited by their specific asset name, and electronic transfers (NEFT/RTGS/UPI/Cheque) always pass through `Bank A/c` (Artificial Personal Account).

## Solution to Exercise 4 – Moderate: Outstanding Salary

- **Date:** March 31, 2026
- **Transaction:** Staff salaries for March 2026 amounting to ₹24,000 are due at financial year-end but remain unpaid.

### 1. Accounts Involved
1. **Salary A/c**
2. **Outstanding Salary A/c**

### 2. Account Classification (Traditional Approach)
| Account Name | Account Type | Subtype |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Salary A/c** | Nominal Account | Expense |
| **Outstanding Salary A/c** | Personal Account | Representative Personal Account |

*(Why `Outstanding Salary A/c` is NOT a Nominal Account:)* Although `Salary A/c` is a Nominal Account, adding the prefix **"Outstanding"** creates a **Representative Personal Account** that represents the group of employees to whom the business owes ₹24,000.

### 3. Step-by-Step Solution & Golden Rules Applied
- **Step 1 (Identify Nature):** Employees have already rendered services during March 2026, so the salary expense belongs to the current financial year (accrual principle), even though cash has not been paid yet. Thus `Cash A/c` is not affected.
- **Step 2 (Apply Nominal Account Rule to Salary A/c):** *Debit all expenses and losses.* Salary of ₹24,000 is an incurred business expense → **Debit Salary A/c ₹24,000**.
- **Step 3 (Apply Personal Account Rule to Outstanding Salary A/c):** *Credit the giver.* Employees (represented by `Outstanding Salary A/c`) are the givers of service to whom ₹24,000 is payable → **Credit Outstanding Salary A/c ₹24,000**.
- **Step 4 (Balance Check):** Total Debit (₹24,000) = Total Credit (₹24,000).

### 4. Correct Journal Entry

| Date | Particulars | L.F. | Debit (₹) | Credit (₹) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2026 Mar 31 | Salary A/c &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dr.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To Outstanding Salary A/c<br>*(Being staff salary for March 2026 due but remaining unpaid)* | | 24,000 | <br>24,000 |

- **Proper Narration:** *(Being staff salary for March 2026 due but remaining unpaid)*
- **Key Takeaway:** Expenses must be recorded in the period in which they are incurred, not just when cash is paid. Unpaid expenses create a Representative Personal Account liability.

### Part A: Annual Depreciation on Shop Furniture

- **Transaction:** Provide annual depreciation at 10% per annum on shop furniture having a book value of ₹80,000 for the full financial year.

#### 1. Arithmetic Calculation
$$\text{Annual Depreciation} = ₹80,000 \times \frac{10}{100} = ₹8,000$$

#### 2. Accounts Involved & Classification (Traditional Approach)
| Account Name | Account Type | Subtype |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Depreciation A/c** | Nominal Account | Expense / Non-cash Loss |
| **Furniture A/c** | Real Account | Tangible Real Account |

#### 3. Step-by-Step Solution & Golden Rules Applied
- **Step 1 (Identify Nature):** Wear and tear over the year permanently reduces the book value of shop furniture by ₹8,000. No cash leaves the business—this is a non-cash expense.
- **Step 2 (Apply Nominal Account Rule to Depreciation A/c):** *Debit all expenses and losses.* Depreciation of ₹8,000 is a business expense/loss → **Debit Depreciation A/c ₹8,000**.
- **Step 3 (Apply Real Account Rule to Furniture A/c):** *Credit what goes out.* Asset value of ₹8,000 effectively goes out (reduces) from the furniture → **Credit Furniture A/c ₹8,000**.
- **Step 4 (Balance Check):** Total Debit (₹8,000) = Total Credit (₹8,000).

#### 4. Correct Journal Entry (Part A)

| Date | Particulars | L.F. | Debit (₹) | Credit (₹) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2026 Mar 31 | Depreciation A/c &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dr.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To Furniture A/c<br>*(Being annual depreciation provided at 10% on shop furniture costing ₹80,000)* | | 8,000 | <br>8,000 |

- **Proper Narration:** *(Being annual depreciation provided at 10% on shop furniture costing ₹80,000)*

### Part B: Goods Withdrawn by Owner for Personal Use

- **Transaction:** Ms. Kiran Sharma withdrew ready-made garments (goods) costing ₹6,500 from the shop for her personal family use.

#### 1. Accounts Involved & Classification (Traditional Approach)
| Account Name | Account Type | Subtype |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Drawings A/c** | Personal Account | Representative Personal Account *(represents proprietor)* |
| **Purchases A/c** | Nominal Account | Expense *(reduction/reversal of purchase cost)* |

*(Why `Sales A/c` is NOT credited:)* Goods taken by the proprietor for personal use are withdrawn at **cost price** with no profit margin. Crediting `Sales A/c` would wrongly overstate business sales revenue. Instead, we credit `Purchases A/c` to reduce the cost of goods purchased for resale.

#### 2. Step-by-Step Solution & Golden Rules Applied
- **Step 1 (Identify Nature):** Under the Business Entity Concept, when the proprietor takes business goods for personal use, it is recorded as **Drawings**, not a sale or business expense.
- **Step 2 (Apply Personal Account Rule to Drawings A/c):** *Debit the receiver.* Ms. Kiran Sharma (represented by `Drawings A/c`) is the receiver of goods worth ₹6,500 → **Debit Drawings A/c ₹6,500**.
- **Step 3 (Apply Nominal Account Rule to Purchases A/c):** *Reversal/Reduction of Expense.* Because `Purchases A/c` (a Nominal Expense Account) was debited when the garments were originally bought for resale, withdrawing ₹6,500 of those goods reduces the business's purchase expense → **Credit Purchases A/c ₹6,500**.
- **Step 4 (Balance Check):** Total Debit (₹6,500) = Total Credit (₹6,500).

#### 3. Correct Journal Entry (Part B)

| Date | Particulars | L.F. | Debit (₹) | Credit (₹) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2026 Mar 31 | Drawings A/c &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Dr.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;To Purchases A/c<br>*(Being ready-made garments costing ₹6,500 withdrawn by proprietor for personal use)* | | 6,500 | <br>6,500 |

- **Proper Narration:** *(Being ready-made garments costing ₹6,500 withdrawn by proprietor for personal use)*
