## Detailed Answer Key and Explanations

### Question 1
- **Correct Option:** **B**
- **Correct Answer:** Because every business transaction is recorded in the Journal first, in chronological order, before being posted to the Ledger.
- **Short Explanation:** A Journal is called the *Book of Original Entry* (or *Prime Entry*) because all financial transactions—both cash and credit—are entered here first, strictly date-wise, before being transferred to the Ledger.

---

### Question 2
- **Correct Option:** **C**
- **Correct Answer:** Patents A/c
- **Short Explanation:** `Patents A/c` is an **Intangible Real Account** because it is a valuable legal asset without physical form. By contrast, `Cash A/c` is a Tangible Real Account, `HDFC Bank A/c` is an Artificial Personal Account, and `Rent A/c` is a Nominal Account.

---

### Question 3
- **Correct Option:** **D**
- **Correct Answer:** Debit all expenses and losses, Credit all incomes and gains.
- **Short Explanation:** Under the Three Golden Rules of Accounting, Nominal Accounts (expenses, losses, incomes, and gains) follow the rule *"Debit all expenses and losses, Credit all incomes and gains."* Option A is for Real Accounts, and Option B is for Personal Accounts.

---

### Question 4
- **Correct Option:** **A**
- **Correct Answer:** Accrued Commission A/c
- **Short Explanation:** Adding the prefix *"Accrued"* to a Nominal Account (`Commission A/c`) converts it into a **Representative Personal Account** representing the party who owes earned commission to the business. `Goodwill A/c` is Intangible Real, `Infosys Ltd. A/c` is Artificial Personal, and `Electricity Charges A/c` is Nominal.

---

### Question 5
- **Correct Option:** **B**
- **Correct Answer:** `Priya Enterprises A/c Dr. ₹28,000 / To Sales A/c ₹28,000`
- **Short Explanation:** Since goods are sold on **credit**, Cash A/c is not affected. `Priya Enterprises A/c` (Artificial Personal Account) is the receiver of goods, so it is debited (*Debit the receiver*). `Sales A/c` (Nominal Account) is business revenue, so it is credited (*Credit all incomes and gains*).

---

### Question 6
- **Correct Option:** **C**
- **Correct Answer:** `Cash A/c Dr. ₹25,000 / To Bank A/c ₹25,000`
- **Short Explanation:** Because the cash is withdrawn for **office use** (not the proprietor's personal use), `Drawings A/c` is not debited. `Cash A/c` (Tangible Real Account) comes into the office cash box (*Debit what comes in*), and `Bank A/c` (Artificial Personal Account) is the giver of funds (*Credit the giver*).

---

### Question 7
- **Correct Option:** **A**
- **Correct Answer:** Debit and Credit
- **Short Explanation:** Under the Modern Accounting Equation Approach (`Assets = Liabilities + Capital`), an **increase in an Asset** is recorded as a **Debit**, whereas an **increase in a Liability** is recorded as a **Credit**.

---

### Question 8
- **Correct Option:** **C**
- **Correct Answer:** `Rent A/c Dr. ₹14,000; Electricity Charges A/c Dr. ₹3,500 / To Bank A/c ₹17,500`
- **Short Explanation:** Both `Rent A/c` (₹14,000) and `Electricity Charges A/c` (₹3,500) are Nominal expense accounts and are debited (*Debit all expenses and losses*). Because payment was made by **bank transfer** (not cash), `Bank A/c` (Artificial Personal Account, giver) is credited with the combined total of `₹14,000 + ₹3,500 = ₹17,500`. Total Debits (₹17,500) = Total Credits (₹17,500).

---

### Question 9
- **Correct Option:** **D**
- **Correct Answer:** `Prepaid Insurance A/c Dr. ₹11,000 / To Insurance A/c ₹11,000`
- **Short Explanation:** Monthly insurance cost = `₹12,000 ÷ 12 = ₹1,000`. By March 31, 2026, only 1 month (March 2026 = ₹1,000) belongs to the current year, leaving **11 months** (`11 × ₹1,000 = ₹11,000`) prepaid for the next financial year. `Prepaid Insurance A/c` (Representative Personal Account) is debited as receiver of the advance, and `Insurance A/c` (Nominal Account) is credited to reduce current-year expense from ₹12,000 to ₹1,000.

---

### Question 10
- **Correct Option:** **B**
- **Correct Answer:** Under the Business Entity Concept, personal expenses of the owner are not business expenses; correct entry: `Drawings A/c Dr. ₹9,000 / To Cash A/c ₹9,000`.
- **Short Explanation:** Under the **Business Entity Concept**, the proprietor and the business are separate entities. Paying the proprietor's daughter's college fees is a personal withdrawal, not a business expense. Therefore, `Drawings A/c` (Representative Personal Account, receiver) is debited, and `Cash A/c` (Tangible Real Account, going out) is credited.
